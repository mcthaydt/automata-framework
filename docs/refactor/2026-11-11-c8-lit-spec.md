# c8 Lit: painted figures lit by the room, per-texel depth under WebGPU, an arch-stable painter

Date: 2026-11-11. Cycle c8-lit. The plan is [`2026-11-11-c8-lit-plan.md`](2026-11-11-c8-lit-plan.md).

The Owner approved this spec and its plan ahead ("auto approve spec and plans", relayed by the coordinator,
`session_01HNdX7KS1hMXgJFNLYmWYFq`). The decisions the coordinator handed down are recorded as theirs; every other choice
below is this spec's call and says so.

## 1. Intent

Cycle c7 ([spec](2026-11-11-c7-fashion-spec.md)) put FigureBuilder's painted figures into AF scenes: D2, painted at 18°,
crisp, on FigureBuilder's own `CellSprite` with a depth per texel. Three things are still wrong, and this cycle fixes them
in one batch.

1. **A painted figure ignores the room's light.** It shows the painter's own key and nothing else: the same picture under a
   pendant at night and on the street at noon. Painted figures replace the mesh figures in EnvironmentBuilder's scenes and
   the clinic, so they have to sit in the room's light. The mesh figures' light (flat and orange under the baked room
   light) is not fixed: that path is being retired.
2. **Under WebGPU a painted figure depth-tests as a flat quad.** FigureBuilder's depth shader bit has a GLSL stage only, so
   a figure cannot hide behind a wall or another figure texel by texel under WebGPU, and AF warns and carries on.
3. **One painted cell differs on arm64.** `nia` on the thin body, direction 0, differs by 2 texels on the Macs, and AF
   carries an `ARCH_OVERRIDES` entry for it. The cause was unknown when c7 merged. It is found here (section 6).

Who it is for: EnvironmentBuilder's scenes and its clinic batch 3 (Ada, the nurse), which draw painted figures with this
cycle's work, and any AF game that puts a painted figure in a lit room.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

Goals:

- A painted figure in a renderer with `lighting` is lit by that room: as bright and as warm as the spot it stands on, with a
  one-step form and a rim on the light's side, never hotter than the ground round it. On WebGL and WebGPU, the same bytes.
- Without `lighting` a painted figure is exactly what it is today: its frame equals FigureBuilder's `compose`, byte for byte.
- A lit frame equals a CPU reference, byte for byte: FigureBuilder's `compose` of the lit cells.
- Under WebGPU a painted figure hides behind a wall and behind another figure texel by texel, equal to `compose`.
- FigureBuilder's painter gives the same cell whatever the last bit of `Math.sin` and `Math.cos`, proven on x64 by a test
  that nudges them, and AF's `ARCH_OVERRIDES` goes once the Macs' run agrees.

Non-goals (the coordinator's):

- The fashion runway (c7 Tasks 9 and 10): on hold until EnvironmentBuilder's glow (#589) merges.
- The clinic's own figures: EnvironmentBuilder's clinic batch 3.
- The mesh figures' light.
- Any change under `packages/3d/src/features/look/`, to world-kit's world builder, or to the clinic spike: they are
  EnvironmentBuilder's coordinator's. This design needs none (section 9 says what happens if that proves wrong).

Anti-goals (a result that works and is still a failure):

- A figure that goes orange under a warm lamp because every colour was multiplied by the light. Each colour stays on its
  own DUEL ramp.
- Lighting that redraws the figure: a smooth gradient, a colour off the palette, bands that swim as the figure walks.
- A GPU pass whose result differs by a texel between engines. Law 3's spirit holds for presentation here: one picture.
- A frame budget spent on light. Eight D2 figures must be lit inside the budget of section 5.
- Loosening or skipping a test to get there, or re-recording a golden per platform.

## 3. What exists today

Read on AF `main` at `5d7f40d3`, FigureBuilder `d6e3c9f` (the pinned submodule) and EnvironmentBuilder `f7c5356`.

- **The painted path** (`extensions/character-kit/src/features/painted/`): `paintedFigure(def)` is a `View3dFactory`. The
  renderer hands every factory the room's light as its fourth argument; `painted-view.ts` names it `_light` and never reads
  it. `painter.ts` paints the cell; `painted-stage.ts` places every figure by the stage contract and shows each cell on a
  `CellSprite(true)` with `texelDepths`. Under anything but WebGL it warns once (`NOT_WEBGL`).
- **The room's light** (`packages/3d/src/shared/render/light/scene-light.ts`, not a look pass): `SceneLight.tint(x, y, z,
  normal?)` is the room's light at a point now, in light units (1 is as authored, capped at 1.6, in steps of 0.1), with the
  lamps and their levels, the occluders, the time of day's ambient, fill and sun already in it (`worldLighting(world,
  time)`). `SceneLight.lighting` is the content it was made from.
- **The haze** (`packages/3d/src/features/look/haze.ts`) is a post pass over the scene's depth, before the DUEL snap. It
  veils whatever wrote depth. The snap leaves an exact DUEL colour alone.
- **FigureBuilder's light** (`src/features/lighting/`): `lightFigure` moves each texel whole steps along its own DUEL row
  (the time's exposure, then each lamp's steps on the texel's normal), tints it in OKLab with `grade` and snaps to DUEL;
  ink stays ink and no texel goes down onto its row's darkest step. It shades across the figure, lamp pool by lamp pool. The
  other path is the light hook (`Figure.light`): the painter drops its key bands, multiplies each texel's colour by a light
  interpolated from the vertices and snaps to DUEL; a figure with a hook set skips the cell cache.
- **EnvironmentBuilder's sprite light** (`src/features/stage/sprite-light.ts`, `src/features/view/expose.ts`): one flat
  light for the whole figure, a form of one step where a texel's normal faces the lights clearly more or less than the
  figure's mean, a rim of one step on the lamps' side of the silhouette, lone stepped texels settled back, ink never
  stepped; then the flat light is taken down in whole steps until the figure is not hotter than the ring of room round it.
  Its form and rim move a texel along its own ramp; its flat light multiplies and snaps.
- **`@automata/3d` already exports a `spriteLight`** (`features/look/sprite-light.ts`): the same rules for the mesh
  figures' parts. It is a look file, it works on parts, not texels, and it does not read the baked room. It is left alone.
- **The stage contract** (`docs/contract.md` in FigureBuilder): `Cell` carries `rgba` (what is drawn), `painted` (unlit),
  `normals` (view space), `depth` and `weave`. `Cell.rgba` may be a lit cell: `cellOf(figure, lit)`. `compose` is the CPU
  reference. Section 4b names the WebGPU limit and the missing WGSL stage.
- **AF #602** (`claude/fix-scene-depth-hazard`, not on `main` yet) ends the look in a colour-only present pass so WebGPU
  frames stay valid after a mesh is added, and repairs the WebGPU scene-depth tests. The WebGPU work here stands on it.

Measured in this session, on this machine (Linux x64, Node 22.23.4), eight D2 base-pair figures walking:

| What | ms a frame (median of 120) |
|---|---|
| Painting (the cell cache warm) | 0.17 |
| `cellOf` for the eight (the colour's hash) | 1.54 |
| `lightFigure`, three lamps, every figure every frame, no cache | 9.18 |

And of the DUEL ramps: a step is ×1.17 in the peak sRGB channel at the median (×1.49 in luminance; steps 1 to 7 of all 32
rows).

## 4. Approaches

Two choices were open: which of FigureBuilder's two colour rules lights the figure, and where the light is computed.

### 4a. The colour rule

| | What it does | Trade |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Whole steps on the painted cell** (chosen) | The flat light is a whole number of steps along each texel's own row; form and rim are one more step; the light's warmth is `grade`'s OKLab tint, snapped to DUEL | Keeps the painter's bands and every hue; the cell cache still works; `lightFigure`'s own rules. The flat light is coarse (a step is ×1.17), which is the look |
| B. The light hook | The painter multiplies albedo by an RGB light per vertex and snaps to DUEL | Drops the painter's key bands (the look gate 1 of c7 approved); a hooked figure skips the cell cache, so every figure repaints every frame; a multiply turns everything the lamp's colour, the mesh path's fault |
| C. EnvironmentBuilder's mix | Form and rim in steps, the flat light multiplied and snapped | The flat multiply leaves the row: a warm lamp walks skin and cloth onto orange rows |

**A, whole steps.** The reason is the anti-goal: a figure must keep its own colours under any lamp, and A is the one rule
that cannot move a texel off its row. It is also FigureBuilder's existing rule (`lightFigure`), so the new function reuses
`onRow`, the rows and `grade` and invents no colour maths. B is ruled out by cost and by the look it loses.

### 4b. Where it is computed

| | What it does | Trade |
|---|---|---|
| **CPU, per cell, in FigureBuilder, cached** (chosen) | A pure function lights `painted` into a new `rgba`; the `CellSprite` draws it as it draws any cell | Byte-exact by construction, the same on WebGL and WebGPU and in every engine; `compose` is already the reference; costs CPU, so it is cached |
| A GPU pass on the sprite | A shader reads colour, normal and a ramp table, steps and snaps | No CPU cost. Needs the ramp lookup, the rim's neighbour taps and the settle pass (a second pass) in GLSL and WGSL; threshold compares on GPU floats differ between drivers, so "equals the CPU reference" would hold only with a tolerance |

**CPU.** The parity goal decides it: a threshold on a GPU float is the kind of flip section 6 exists to remove. The cost is
bounded by a cache: a lit cell depends on the painted cell and on a small quantised light (a step count, a tint in eighths,
a direction in sixteenths), so a figure standing still, or walking through even light, lights each walk frame once.

## 5. The design: a painted figure lit by the room

### 5a. In FigureBuilder: `lightSprite`

A new pure function beside `lightFigure`, in `src/features/lighting/sprite.ts`, exported from `src/index.ts`:

```ts
/** The room's light on one figure, in the figure's lighting frame (contract section 3: x to the screen's right, y up,
 * z toward the camera; world turned by Ry(−azimuth)). */
export interface SpriteLighting {
  /** The room's light where the figure stands, RGB in light units: 1 is as painted. */
  flat: V3;
  /** Where the light comes from: unit directions toward it, each with its strength in light units. May be empty. */
  lamps: { dir: V3; strength: number }[];
  /** Whole steps taken off the flat light (0 or more): the host's exposure. */
  exposure?: number;
  style?: SpriteStyle;
}
export function flatSteps(flat: V3): number;
export function lightSprite(f: LitSource, L: SpriteLighting): Uint8ClampedArray;
```

The rules, in order. Every number is a named field of `SPRITE_STYLE`, a starting value tuned at the gate (section 8).

1. **Never stepped**: an empty texel, ink (`0x000000`), `RAMP_OUTLINE`, and a texel on its row's step 0 (the painter's
   family darks). They come out as they went in. This is `lightFigure`'s floor rule.
2. **The flat light, in steps.** `flatSteps(flat) = clamp(round(ln(peak(flat)) / ln(1.17)), −3, 2)`: the measured ratio of a
   DUEL step, so a light of 1 is no step, 1.6 is two up (clamped), 0.5 is three down (clamped). `shift = flatSteps(flat) −
   exposure`.
3. **The form.** With lamps: each texel's normal is turned from view space into the lighting frame (`viewToWorld` at the
   cell's pitch); `litBy(n) = Σ max(0, n · dir) × strength`; the figure's mean of it over texels that have a normal is
   `facing`. A texel whose `litBy` clears `facing` by `threshold × Σ strength` (0.22) takes +1, one under by as much −1.
4. **The rim.** A filled texel with an empty 4-neighbour whose outward direction, in the picture plane, takes at least `rim`
   (0.35) of the lamps' strength takes +1 instead. A lamp within `rimSide` (0.25) of the view axis rims nothing.
5. **Settle.** A texel stepped unlike every same-coloured 4-neighbour (two or more of them) takes theirs.
6. **The step.** For a texel on row `r`, step `s ≥ 1`: `clamp(s + shift + form, 1, 7)` on row `r`. Never row-hopping, never
   step 0.
7. **The warmth.** Where the flat light has a colour (its chroma over a small floor), `grade(colour, tint, amount)` tints
   toward it in OKLab and snaps to DUEL, the amount in eighths, as `lightFigure` tints toward a lamp. A grey light tints
   nothing. This is the one place a texel may leave its row, by FigureBuilder's own sanctioned rule, and `across` in `grade`
   already holds a hue across the wheel back.
8. **Fabric.** A tiled texel lights its ground (`weave.ground`) by rules 2 to 6, takes no tint, and the tile is laid after
   with `tiled`, exactly as `lightFigure` does, so a hatch and its ground never part.

It is deterministic and allocates one output. It reads nothing but its arguments.

The stage gets it as a third light path: `StageOptions.sprite?: (actor, at) => SpriteLighting | null`, beside `lighting`
and `lights`; more than one of the three throws, as two do today. So `stageFrame` and `compose` are the CPU reference for a
sprite-lit frame inside FigureBuilder too, and `npm run view-check` gains a lit case.

The contract: section 3 gains the third path, the changelog a line, `tests/api.test.ts` the new names. Adding names breaks
no consumer, so `STAGE_VERSION` stays 1. A new golden, `tests/golden/sprite.json` (the base pair under four lights: none,
a warm lamp from the left, a dim grey room, noon), is recorded once. The four existing goldens do not move: nothing they
draw calls the new function.

### 5b. In AF: the room's light for one figure

A new pure module, `extensions/character-kit/src/features/painted/room-light.ts`, turns the renderer's `SceneLight` into a
`SpriteLighting`. It reads only `SceneLight.tint`, so the lamps and their levels now, the occluders, the time of day's
ambient, fill and sun are all in it without the kit knowing any of them, and nothing in `packages/3d` changes.

- **The flat light**: `tint(x, y + CHEST, z)` with no normal, `CHEST = 1.2` m: the room's light at the chest.
- **Where it comes from**: six probes at the chest, `tint` with the normals ±x, ±y, ±z. The difference of each pair's peak
  channel is the light's lean on that axis. The three leans make one resultant: its direction, turned into the figure's
  lighting frame by Ry(−azimuth), is the one lamp; its length is the strength. Two equal lamps either side cancel, and the
  figure is lit flat, which is right. Under the resultant's floor (`0.05`) there is no lamp: no form, no rim.
- **Exposure toward the ring**: four probes on the floor round the feet (0.75 m out, normal up) give the ring's light.
  The figure's steps are held within one step of the ring's: `exposure = max(0, flatSteps(chest) − flatSteps(ring) − 1)`.
  EnvironmentBuilder measures the drawn frame's luma round the figure; AF's frame is on the GPU and a readback per figure
  per frame is neither affordable nor engine-stable, so AF reads the room's light on the ground instead. That is this
  spec's call, and the gate pictures judge it.
- **Quantised**: the direction to sixteenths a component, the strength to the room's light step (0.1). The quantised light
  is the cache key.

`painter.ts` lights the cell: with a `SceneLight`, it calls `lightSprite` on the painted figure and makes the cell with
`cellOf(figure, lit)`; without one it does what it does today. A cache per figure holds the lit cells by
`<painted cell's key>|<light key>`, at most 32 (about 3 MB a figure at D2), the oldest dropped. `painted-view.ts` passes the
factory's `light` through. `PaintedFigureOptions` gains `lit?: boolean` (default: true when the renderer has `lighting`), so
a game can keep a figure unlit.

The haze needs no code: it is a pass over depth, and a painted texel writes its depth. A test asserts it.

### 5c. The budget

Eight D2 figures, lit, walking through the base interior: **at most 2 ms a frame at the median and 6 ms at the worst
frame** for the light (probes, `lightSprite`, the lit cell's hash), on the machine that runs the bench, measured by a bench
in `extensions/character-kit/tests/bench/` and written into the gate's `measurements.json`. If the bench misses it, the
plan stops at the coordinator's checkpoint with the numbers; it does not lower the density or skip frames.

## 6. The design: per-texel depth under WebGPU

Read in `@pixi/3d` 0.1.0-beta.2: every WGSL fragment entry returns a bare `@location(0) vec4<f32>`. A shader bit fills
slots inside that function, so no bit alone can write `@builtin(frag_depth)`: the entry point's return type has to change.
Materials compile from a `ShaderBitTemplate`, which is public.

- **In FigureBuilder** (`src/features/view/cell-sprite.ts`): the depth-writing material compiles from a template of its
  own whose WGSL fragment entry returns a struct, `@location(0) color` and `@builtin(frag_depth) depth`, and `cellDepthBit`
  gains its `gpu` stage: the same float texture, the same `depthProj`, the same bias, and the window depth the camera's own
  projection gives. WebGPU's clip z runs 0 to 1, where WebGL's runs −1 to 1 and is halved; which one the camera's matrix
  holds under each renderer is read from the matrix `StageView` already passes, and the parity test, not the comment,
  decides. `StageView` reports `perTexelDepth === true` under WebGPU and stops warning. `npm run view-check` runs its stage
  cases under WebGPU as well as WebGL and requires 0 pixels of difference from `compose` in each.
- **If the template cannot be swapped** on the flat material through the public API, the plan stops and tells the
  coordinator what `@pixi/3d` would need; it does not patch the tarball.
- **In AF**: `painted-stage.ts` drops `NOT_WEBGL` and the warning. `painted-depth.test.ts`'s WebGPU case stops asserting a
  warning and asserts what the WebGL cases assert: the wall case and the overlap case equal `compose`, 0 pixels differing
  outside true crossings (the rule already in that file). The contract's section 4b loses its known limit.
- **AF #602.** The WebGPU tests need a valid WebGPU frame after a mesh is added, which is #602. The plan builds on `main`
  once #602 is there. If it has not merged when the AF tasks start, the branch merges `claude/fix-scene-depth-hazard` (a
  merge, so it dissolves when `main` has it), and the pull request says so and is not marked ready before #602 is on
  `main`.

## 7. The design: an arch-stable painter

### 7a. What was found

The flip is reproduced on x64. Nudging `Math.sin` and `Math.cos` by one unit in the last place, at random, while `nia` on
the thin body is painted at direction 0, gives the Macs' md5 exactly (`259c7a37…`, the two texels at row 74, columns 55 and
56, `#23324d` to `#181f2f`) in 16 of 40 seeds; 23 give x64's (`71301072…`), and one gives a third picture, 13 texels
different. Nudging `sin` alone or `cos` alone does it; `atan2`, `pow`, `exp`, `log`, `tan`, `acos`, `asin`, `atan` and
`cbrt` alone never do (24 seeds each). So the Macs' picture is one last-bit rounding of `sin` or `cos` away from x64's,
which is what a maths library built with fused multiply-add gives.

Traced with a nudged seed against the plain run, at those two texels:

- the tone cut (`f > th[0]`, `paint.ts`) is not at its threshold: `f` is 0.934 and 0.873 against 0.85, and under the nudge
  `f` itself falls to 0.850 and 0.701;
- `f` falls because the smoothed normal changes, and that because the G-buffer's own normals change, grossly, in the texels
  round them: at (56, 76) from (−0.57, 0.36, **−0.74**), facing away, to (0.02, −0.17, 0.99);
- the part, the material and the depth (to five places) are the same in both runs, the supersample's vote is 9 of 9 for one
  part, and the cast shadow, the crevice and the smoothing's depth test are all far from their thresholds (measured).

**The owner is `rasterize` in `src/features/paint/raster.ts`**: the depth test
`if (z + … <= depth[k]) continue`. One part of the tee has two surfaces in the same place with opposed normals (an inner
and an outer face). Their interpolated depths are equal to the last bit or two, so which triangle wins a sample is decided
by rounding; the part and the depth are the same either way, but the normal and the place are the winner's, and the key
light on a normal facing away is dark. The painter's thresholds are sound; the tie in the raster is the flip.

### 7b. The fix

In `rasterize`, the comparison is made robust and the tie is decided by a rule, not by rounding:

- depths are compared **quantised** to a fixed grid (2⁻²⁰ m, about a micrometre): two surfaces a few last bits apart land
  on the same grid value, except across a grid line, which a last-bit difference crosses about once in 10¹⁰ samples;
- on an equal quantised depth **within one part**, the triangle whose interpolated normal faces the camera more (the
  greater z) wins, whichever was drawn first: the outer face, which is also the right picture;
- across parts an equal quantised depth keeps the first drawn, as `<=` does today, and the cloth-over-leg rule (`LAYER`,
  `over`) is unchanged. The stored depth stays the unquantised value.

No fused-multiply-sensitive expression is left at a threshold by this: the only arch-dependent inputs are the last bits of
the vertices, and the quantised compare absorbs them.

### 7c. The proof without an arm64 machine

A test that nudges the maths, in FigureBuilder (`tests/features/paint/ulp.test.ts`): `Math.sin` and `Math.cos` (and the
nine others, so a later use is covered) return their value a last bit up, down or unchanged, by a seeded generator. Under
each of 40 seeds, every cell it paints must equal the un-nudged cell, byte for byte:

- a unit case straight on `rasterize`: two coincident triangles of one part with opposed normals, each drawn first in
  turn, depths a last bit apart either way: the front-facing normal wins every time;
- the base pair, 8 directions, standing and one walk frame, at D2.

And in AF (`extensions/character-kit/tests/unit/fashion/ulp.test.ts`): the same harness over the runway golden's cells,
`nia/thin/0` first. Before the fix that test fails with the Macs' md5, which is the evidence that it tests the right thing.

This proves stability against last-bit differences in the maths library, which is what the evidence says arm64 is. It
does not prove there is no other arch difference; only a Mac run does. AF's `checks` job runs on the Macs (`af-mac`), so
the pull request that bumps the submodule and drops `ARCH_OVERRIDES` is that run.

### 7d. Goldens, said plainly

- **FigureBuilder's four goldens on x64**: predicted not to move. The base pair wear painted-on blocks and no garment, so
  no doubled surface is expected. The plan measures it: `npm test` with the goldens untouched. If any golden moves, the plan
  stops, shows the diff to the coordinator, and a re-record is a named commit that says why (the rule in FigureBuilder's
  `CLAUDE.md`). It is not re-recorded quietly.
- **AF's runway golden on x64** (`runway-cells.json`, 112 md5s): **may move, and `nia/thin/0` is expected to.** Its x64
  picture today has an inner face winning some samples. After the fix the outer face wins, which may be x64's picture, the
  Macs', or neither. The plan counts the cells that move, looks at the expected, actual and diff image of each, and
  re-records with `goldens:runway` in one commit that names the count and the cause. The golden is made from
  FigureBuilder's showcase on the pinned builder, so a moved cell is the builder's change, not the kit's.
- **`ARCH_OVERRIDES`** is deleted in the same pull request, with `expectedFitMd5`'s arch argument. If the Macs then
  disagree, the override does not come back by default: `cell-miss.ts` prints the cell, and the plan's fallback is to trace
  it the way section 7a did.

## 11. Risks

- **The six-probe resultant is too blunt** (two lamps on one side at different heights read as one). The gate shows it; the
  fallback is one lamp per axis pair, still through `tint` alone.
- **`@pixi/3d` will not take a custom template on the flat material.** Then WebGPU depth waits on `@pixi/3d`, the other two
  parts ship, and the contract's limit stays, restated.
- **The raster rule moves more of AF's runway golden than expected.** Every moved cell is looked at; a cell that gets worse
  is a finding for the coordinator, not a re-record.
- **CI**: each push cancels the runs in flight and a conflicted pull request gets none; #592, #593 and #594 are known reds
  and are not this cycle's.

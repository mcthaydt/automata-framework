## Why this task exists

The Sector toolbar in the mockups has Split and Merge beside Polygon and Rectangle, and the ED3D.3 plan left both edits to ED3D.6 "with the tools whose rules they are (which loop is cut, which side keeps the id)". Those rules are what this plan settles. Drawing a room, then carving a closet out of it or opening a wall between two rooms, is the other half of laying out a dungeon (spec §17): without them the only way to change a room's shape is to delete it and draw it again, which loses its walls, doors and the portals of its neighbours.

## Decisions this plan makes (each vetoable by the Owner through the Orchestrator)

1. **A cut is two points on the outline of one sector** (`from`, `to`, each at a corner or on a wall within a millimetre, as `insertCorner` takes a point). The edit makes the corners it needs by the vertex edits' rule (so a portal's reverse is split with it and a door or window a cut would run through is refused), and cuts between them.
2. **The larger half keeps the id**, the smaller is the new sector (`id` in the args). Equal halves: the half that runs from `from` to `to` along the outline keeps it. Things stand where they stand, so nothing else follows a sector by id; the sector a neighbour's portal names is what must follow, and does (decision 4).
3. **The new sector is a copy of the old one with its new loop**: every member the edit does not know (style, textures, light, heights, `view`, Last Light's `zone`, a newer file's members) is carried by spreading. Holes go to the half that holds them; a cut that crosses or touches a hole is refused.
4. **The cut becomes a portal pair**: the kept half's cut wall says `next` the new sector, the new half's says `next` the kept one. A wall that moved to the new sector and led into a neighbour has its reverse in the neighbour retargeted to the new id. A reverse that reaches the sector through a wall preset gets a `next` of its own, which wins over the preset's, so the preset and the other walls that use it stay as they were (a preset that leads into the sector is in `pinned` anyway, so this is a safety net).
5. **A sector with something tied to its walls is not cut or merged**: `SectorAccess.pinned` lists, for Last Light, the openings that name the room, the wall presets whose `next` is the room and the things that name one of its walls by number; the AF sector has none. The edits refuse with those sentences. Reversible, and removed when the things model renumbers wall references.
6. **Merge keeps the first sector's id and members and drops the second**: floor and ceiling must agree (a sentence names the one that does not); the light, style, textures and `view` of the kept sector win. Every other sector's walls that led into the dropped one now lead into the kept one. The union loses the corners that the cut made: a corner at either end of the removed stretch goes when its two walls are in line and say the same (the `joinWalls` rule). Where the two walls are a portal, the corner goes only together with the matching corner on the far side (whose two walls then lead into the kept sector); when the far side cannot be joined the corner stays on both. So a split followed by a merge is the level it began as, up to the corner the outline starts at: the union keeps the kept sector's own corner order, from its first corner that is still a corner. Holes of both sectors are carried (a sector that fills a hole of the other has no wall in common with its outline, and is refused as such).
7. **Merge writes the union in the kept sector's direction.** A loop may be written either way round, and a neighbour drawn the other way would otherwise put its walls backwards: it is turned round (`reversedLoop`), and a wall turned round keeps what it says with the places along it measured from its new start (a door, a window, a hatch, a pilaster's start and end). A texture pan along the wall is left as written, so a mirrored wall's pattern may run the other way.
8. **Merge refuses a stretch of wall that is not one piece**: two sectors that meet in two places would leave a hole (a sentence says so). A shared wall is one that runs between the same two corners in both sectors, in opposite directions once both are written the same way round; walls that only overlap are not shared. A shared wall that leads into a third sector is refused.

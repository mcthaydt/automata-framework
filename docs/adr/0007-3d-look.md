# ADR 0007: The 3D look is drawn by passes on Pixi 3D

- Status: Accepted under the standing rule (Owner, 2026-10-31: "Reminder that everything is auto approved and accepted, do not wait on me to do any approvals"); the choice is provisional on the GPU runner's frame numbers (see Consequences)
- Date: 2026-11-07
- Spike: [`spikes/3d-look/`](../../spikes/3d-look/README.md), run on `@pixi/3d` 0.1.0-beta.2

## Context
The look is DUEL pixel art: a low-resolution frame drawn nearest-neighbour, light cut into whole steps along each colour's
ramp, every pixel snapped to the palette and an outline where depth jumps ([3D spec](../refactor/2026-11-07-3d-slice-spec.md) §6).
Two ways to get it were named: (a) *passes*, drawn live by Pixi 3D, and (b) *bake*, offline sprite cells and
textures drawn as billboards. The spec's rule: (a) if it holds the phone budget with the camera turning; otherwise (a)
for the world and (b) for figures. If the beta took no custom material, (a) would quantise in the post filter alone.

## What was checked
1. **Custom material: yes.** `createLightingBit` builds a lighting bit that replaces `phongLightingBit` in a `PhongMaterial`
   subclass's descriptor. The spike's bit cuts the summed light (direct plus ambient) into five steps before it multiplies the
   albedo. 92 meshes of base-3d's room and Wick took it and drew with no console error.
2. **Low-resolution target drawn nearest-neighbour: yes, and core already has it.** The letterbox's composite target
   (`render/letterbox/composite-target.ts`) is a `RenderTexture` at resolution 1 with `scaleMode: 'nearest'`, and a `View3D` given
   the game's logical size draws into it. A field with a short side of 180 is therefore a `size` the game declares; no new
   target is needed.
3. **Post filter: yes.** `PostEffect` takes `effect(uv)` in GLSL and WGSL, with `sampleColor`, `sampleDepth`, `linearDepth` and
   `texel()`. The spike's effect snaps to the nearest of DUEL's 256 colours and draws depth edges. The WGSL twin was not run:
   Chromium here has no WebGPU, so WebGL2 and WebGPU parity is measured on the GPU runner in 8.2.

## What was measured
Chromium (software WebGL2, headless shell), 844 × 390 viewport, base-3d's room with Wick, the camera turning (E) and Wick
walking (D) for 8 s; frame intervals from `requestAnimationFrame`. Recorded, not judged: software drawing has no phone's
fill-rate or bandwidth. Raw files: `spikes/out/*.json` (git-ignored, regenerated by `measure.mjs`).

| Mode | Field | Median | p95 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `full`: today, Phong | 844 × 390 | 50.0 ms | 100.0 ms |
| `lowres`: nearest-upscaled field | 389 × 180 | 33.4 ms | 50.1 ms |
| `passes`: lowres + stepped-light material + DUEL snap + depth outline | 389 × 180 | 50.0 ms | 66.8 ms |
| `bake`: lowres + unlit flat meshes + one billboard figure | 389 × 180 | 33.3 ms | 50.0 ms |

In software every pixel costs, so the low-resolution field alone is worth about a third of the frame, and the passes give
back about half of that. The spike snaps with a loop over DUEL's 256 colours per pixel, which 8.2 keeps (a `PostEffect` declares no extra sampler, so a
look-up texture needs a custom `Pass3D`): the GPU runner says whether the loop is worth replacing. The frames show the intended look (stepped shading, DUEL
colours, outlines). No GPU, phone or iPad was available in this session, so the phone budget (33 ms) and the iPad budget
(16.7 ms) are not yet measured.

## Decision
**(a) passes**, for the world and for figures, by the spec's own default: the beta takes a custom material and a post effect,
the low-resolution target costs nothing new, and the passes' cost over the low-resolution field is the one number the
spike could not settle without a GPU. (b) is not built. It would add a bake step, a sprite atlas and eight-direction cells
per figure, and it loses the figure's live lighting, which the editors' time-of-day control needs.

## Consequences
- 8.2 builds `features/look/`: a stepped-light lighting bit and material, a DUEL snap filter and an outline
  filter as GLSL + WGSL pairs, `targetSize` for the low-resolution field, `spriteLight` for figures and `addLookPass` for an
  extension's own pass. The scene's sky and horizon stay core.
- The 3D stress page (a later task) measures `passes` with Wick and every pass on at the phone (33 ms) and iPad (16.7 ms)
  profiles, with exclusive GPU access. **If the figures miss while the world holds, figures move to (b)** (a Node bake
  script and billboards): that is a view-side change only, because the sim, the content and the level format do not name a
  look. If the world misses too, the field's short side drops below 180 first.
- No sim, content or level-format change either way. The look is a view concern: a game that sets no look draws as it does today.

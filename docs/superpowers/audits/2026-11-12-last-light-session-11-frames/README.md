## Item 2: the wainscot from the floor-tile photograph (Floor Tiles 08)

[`strip-item2-old-before-after.png`](strip-item2-old-before-after.png): old, ours at the end of session 10, ours now. Zoom of the band: [`zoom-item2-band-before-after.png`](zoom-item2-band-before-after.png); changed pixels: [`diff-item2-before-after.png`](diff-item2-before-after.png).

- **No new photograph was needed.** Floor Tiles 08 (Poly Haven `floor_tiles_08`, CC0, Rob Tuytel) is already the kit's `floorTiles` (`assets/photos/photos.json`), which session 9 used for the floor.
- **The photograph holds five tiles a side, not six** (a column profile of `floorTiles.png` repeats at 38.4 of its 192 px). A 15 cm tile is `scale = 0.5`. The same count means the floor, at `scale = 1.2`, is 36 cm tiles, not the 30 cm its comment and test say (item 3 corrects it).
- **Why the joint was lost before:** at the kit's 32 texels a metre a 15 cm tile is 4.8 texels, and its joint is a tenth of a texel's width, so it averages away (the tile came out a flat step). `reads = { at = 64 }` makes the tile at 64 texels a metre (9.6 texels a tile, a one-texel joint). `contrast` 0.04 (a step per 0.04 of OKLab lightness, `range` -4 to 1) is the largest that keeps the joint: at 0.1 the joints fall inside one step and vanish; at 0.025 the glaze mottles into camouflage.
- **Test first:** `tests/unit/features/room/tiles.test.ts` reads the committed tile and asks for a tile 15 cm across (its width in metres over five), the floor-tile photograph, and column and row mean-light profiles that repeat at one tile (correlation above 0.4 on both axes, the tile's joints lining up with each other). It failed first against the old wall-tile swatch for the right reason (a tile 0.37 m across).
- **At the play camera the grid is faint.** The wainscot shows in the east wall only behind the counter, lit by the lamp and seen obliquely: 4,016 pixels of the frame changed. High-passed column profile of the band (x 770 to 930, y 305 to 332; smoothed 4 px, minus a 24 px mean), std as a share of the band's mean luma: 3.7 % before, 4.7 % now (the old game's tile row, where it shows under the cork, has clear dark joints about 20 px apart). The joint is one to two ramp steps below the glaze (luma 0.83 to 0.9 of it), the old grout `#625d54` is 0.47 of the tile: darker joints need a ramp step lower than the sand ramp's range allows without the mottle; not closed.

## Item 5 (first half): the window glass

**Cause:** `buildWorld` draws a window's panes `glassDay` (DUEL sky step 5, bright cyan) unless `options.night` is true, and the game built the ward without it (`worldLighting` was told it is night, `buildWorld` was not): a swatch in the level's window `glass` changed nothing because the pane's glass is not the window's `glass` field. **Fix:** `buildWorld(LEVEL, LIBRARY, { night: true })` in `room.ts`; `tests/unit/features/room/world.test.ts` asserts every pane is `glassNight` (red first on `glassDay`).

## Item 4: the mesh pipeline table

**The count is 41 files, not 62:** `public/models/*.bin` holds 41 models (`PROVENANCE.md` lists each), drawn for **57 props** of the level (several props share a model: waste bins, radiators, stock). Read from the code at this commit (`packages/3d/src/app/world-view.ts`, `shared/level/models-of.ts`, `af-sector-2.schema.ts`, `load-models.ts`) and the level file; not each one measured by a picture.

| Rule | Where it stands | Whose |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Enters through the content compiler and manifest | **No.** The `.bin` files sit in `public/models` and are named by the level's props (`model`), listed for the renderer by `modelsOf(level)` and recorded in `PROVENANCE.md` by hand; the content compiler has no kind for them, so no content hash or manifest covers them, and a changed model moves no hash. A missing file fails boot by name (`ModelInvalid`), which is the only check. | The framework's: a model asset kind (compiled, hashed, in the manifest) is a proposal; not built |
| 2. Placed and fitted by its blockout | **Yes, 57 of 57.** Every model prop has a blockout (`use` or `kind`) that owns its place, turn, footprint, height and collider; the model is fitted to it (largest uniform scale inside the blockout's box, on its base, centred). `fit` overrides on 16 props only (14 quarter turns, `privacy-screen` 22.92 degrees, `pendant-lamp` scale 0.4443 and offset); the other 41 take the default fit. | The game's data, fine |
| 3. Takes the baked light and hides with its room | **Yes.** In a lit scene a model's material is the unlit texture with the room's baked light in its vertices (`scene.light.bake`, `textured`, its blockout's swatch as the floor probe's albedo, its blockout's pieces never shadowing it); it joins its prop's sector node for `showRooms`; a wall-hung model follows its wall's cut (`occluders.face`). Not run for each model here. | Fine |
| 4. Sampled nearest, and on DUEL | **Nearest: yes** (session 9: model textures were linear, now nearest, test first). **On DUEL: only at the end.** A model's texture colours are the old game's own (the bake never touches them), so they reach the palette only through the final DUEL snap pass, not through the ramp the swatches' photo tiles use: the models are the one part of the picture that is not photo-made through the kit (the Owner: "3D assets can carry over, but fit the 3D assets into our pipeline"). A model texture to the DUEL palette on load (a snapped, nearest texture) is the pipeline step missing. | The framework's: a model-import step that indexes the texture to the palette; a proposal, not built |
| 5. Shown by the 3D level editor at its blockout, or logged in the editor-gaps ledger | **At its blockout:** the level editor draws the generated pieces (the blockout) and never the model (`blockout` mode is the editor's draw); the level codec keeps `model` and `fit` in the file because they are schema fields. Not run in the editor in this session; no row of the editor-gaps ledger names the models. | A ledger row is owed: "Levels 3D draws a model prop at its blockout and cannot set `model` or `fit`" |

**What is the game's and was fixed here:** nothing; the table's four rows that are not fine are the framework's. **Not done:** a picture of every model against the old game's (the strip would be 41 panels).

## Decisions (open to the Owner's veto)

1. **Layout.** `packages/figures/src/motion/`, one responsibility a file, each under 600 lines: `motion-state.ts` (the state, `ArmHold`, `Evaluated`), `gait-path.ts` (the foot path and `moveDir`), `stands.ts` (`CHAIN`, `openings`, `place`, `crossed`, `standHolds`, `fits`, `standFor`), `base.ts` (`relaxed`; torso, head, arms), `legs.ts` (the feet into IK), `hems.ts` (`placeHems`), `hold.ts` (`holdOf`, `applyHold`, `evaluateMotion`), `clock.ts` (`poseClock`, `turnStep`, `headLead`, `shufflePhase`, `wrapAngle`), `hair-spring.ts` (`hairStep`, `headNod`), `gait-of.ts` (`gaitOf`, `hairOf`, `DEFAULT_HAIR`). `gait-path.ts` also holds the ways a walk plays (`wayOf`, `backWeight`, `halfTrack`, `footOn`, decision 12).
2. **`ArmHold`.** The source's runtime `Hold` (a hand place in a joint's space, a turn or an aim, a pole) is `ArmHold`, because `Hold` already names the item library's content shape (M3.5a). `holdOf(item, skeleton, side)` turns an item's measures into one (the source's `item().hold`).
3. **`evaluateMotion`, not `evaluate`.** The source's `evaluate(s, gait, st)` (the base, a stand's placed hands, the item's hold) is `evaluateMotion`. `evaluate(skeleton, def, state, time, library)` is the spec's M3.6 entry (§4.4.5) and keeps its name; it will run `base()`, the clips and `solve()`, then the holds of this row (step 6) and the hair spring (step 7).
4. **`moveDir`.** `MotionState.moveDir` (radians of travel against the facing, 0 when left out). The feet's paths run along the travel line: the stride vector is the forward vector turned by `moveDir` about +y (the rig's yaw convention, so +π/2 is toward the figure's left). Behind the back band (decision 12) the cycle plays backwards (phase `1 − p`) along `moveDir − π`, so a half-turn is exactly the forward walk played in reverse (F-h): the planted foot slides forward under the body, the arms still swing against the legs, and the whole pose at phase `p` equals the forward pose at `1 − p`. The feet's paths, their roll, and a foot's lead over its hip (legs and hems) read `moveDir`; the hips' turn, the lean and the arm swing are the source's.
5. **The chin lift needs no camera.** The source scales the gait's `chin` by the face's turn to the camera (`toward`, a sprite cheat). Here it is the gait's attitude at full weight (`toward = 1`), as the source gives a figure drawn facing its camera.
6. **Presentation time.** `poseClock(gait, time, { still })` gives the stepped walk phase (`walkPoses` a cycle, in [0, 1), from integer steps of the time given, so the walk repeats exactly after one cycle, F-c; a caller that wants a walk to open on its first pose passes the time since the walk began), the stepped idle time (looped at `idleLoop`, held `idlePoses`; 0 while `still`, the source's `idleT = still ? 0 : steppedT` for a turn on the spot, its other reason, an action, being a clip's in M3.6), and the shuffle's beat. `turnStep(gait, shown, facing)` is one pose step of the turn on the spot; `headLead(gait, shown, facing)` the head's lead, clamped to `headLead` with no 30° steps. All pure: the view (M3.5d) and `evaluate` (M3.6) keep the shown facing.
7. **The hair spring is a pure step.** `hairStep(dynamics, spring, input, dt)` returns the next `{ pitch, roll, vPitch, vRoll }` with the source's semi-implicit Euler in 1/120 s substeps (frame-rate independent). Its input is what the source read from the figure: moving, the walk phase, the speed (the source passes the gait's tuned speed; the caller may pass the sim's), the yaw rate, the head's nod (`headNod(world, yaw)`, the head's up vector tipped along the facing) and the head's tilt. No rounding: the source's tenth-radian rounding served its sprite cache.
8. **Stands and fallbacks.** `standFor(skeleton, built, id, stands)` returns the first of the stand and its `fallback`s that the dressed figure suits (`fits`: every placed hand finds its first-choice opening among `built.anchors`, and crossed arms only in sleeves slim at the forearm), else `null` (the gait's relaxed stand). `standHolds(skeleton, stand, surface, anchors)` places the hands on `built.anchors`, the surface the dressed parts' `reach`; with none, the body's own openings.
9. **Goldens from the source.** FigureBuilder `a1f7246` runs on Node 24 through the committed generator `packages/figures/scripts/figurebuilder-motion-goldens.ts` (`node packages/figures/scripts/figurebuilder-motion-goldens.ts <FigureBuilder checkout> [--check]`, a knip entry; it copies FigureBuilder's `src/` and AF's testing files to a temp folder made loadable, as M3.5b's generator does). `testing/__goldens__/figurebuilder-motion.json` (one state a line, 12 significant digits) holds each joint's whole world transform (`testing/motion-fingerprint.ts`: its position and its rotation as a quaternion, compared either sign) for `base()` and `evaluate()` on Wick's and the keeper's bodies (the golden outfits' sliders) in every fixture gait (the figure's own in all 13 `GOLDEN_STATES`, the others in `GOLDEN_SOME_STATES`), every stand, every item's hold in both hands; `standHolds` and the stood pose for every stand on every golden outfit's dressed anchors and surface; and 40 steps of the hair spring (`Figure.prototype.swingHair` on a stand-in `this` holding only the fields it reads). FigureBuilder runs with `view: 0`, no `lookUp` and no `pitch` (decision 5, the cut sprite cheats), and walking at t 0.5 (decision 10); library ids are turned into AF's kebab-case; the port matches to 1e-9.
10. **The twitch is idle's.** The source twitches the head on the idle clock even while walking, so a twitchy gait (the elderly, the uncanny) never repeats after one cycle. Here the twitch runs only standing and turning (the spec lists it with idle), so the walk repeats exactly (F-c). The goldens' walking states sit at t 0.5, where no twitch falls, so they hold either way.
11. **Strict TypeScript.** As 5a and 5b: the source's formulas and their order, array reads through `at`, readonly content types read as the source's.
12. **The strafe step and the back band** (M3.5c review, 2026-10-30). *A side step that never crosses:* across the body the stride shortens to `SIDE_STRIDE` (0.5) of the forward one at a pure strafe (scaled by `1 − 0.5·|sin line|`, the whole stride vector, so its direction stays the forward vector turned by `moveDir`), the feet's middle lines move out toward the stand's `SIDE_TRACK` (0.75 of the hip width, at least the gait's own track) and apart by the stride's whole sideways span. Each foot's path then reaches at most half that span either side of its middle line, so the feet come no closer than the track: the leading foot steps out and the trailing one closes, a step-together, never one over the other. The heel-to-toe roll is kept only for the stride's forward share (`|cos line|`): a side step pushes off the foot's edge, so the foot stays flat. *Continuity:* the reversed cycle is not chosen at the strafe line any more. Forward of the back band (cos moveDir ≥ −0.4, out to about 114°) the forward cycle plays along the turned line, behind it (cos ≤ −0.6, from about 127°) the reversed one, and in the band the two poses are blended (each joint's turn by a normalised lerp, its shift linearly, by `smooth` of the band's position). The pose is continuous all round the circle, so a lock-on circle strafe at ±π/2 plays one cycle with no pop; the remaining cost sits in the band (114° to 127°, a back diagonal), where a planted foot may slide a little because poses, not contacts, are blended. *Along the line:* a foot's lead over its hip (the strike's soft knee) and a hem's fore and aft are measured along the line the feet run, and the hips' draw-in toward the feet uses the feet's own track, so a strafe rides its planted feet as a forward walk does.

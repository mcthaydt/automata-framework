## Decisions (open to the Owner's veto)

1. **Layout.** Five folders under `packages/figures/src/`, each file one responsibility and under 600 lines after `pnpm format` (110 columns): `parts/` (mesh types, primitives, skin weights, reach, texels), `garments/` (body, head, hands and shoes, face marks, locks, the classic garments, `dress`), `hair/` (skull, sweep, shell, tones, pieces, tail, cap), `streetwear/` (loft, the body under and skinning, tops' shared shapes, layering, each garment family, the registry) and `build/` (`build`, anchors and clearance, items). The modules that reference the registry (`underneath`, `topOf`, `reachable`) only call it inside functions, so the registry's imports of the garment builders form no load-time cycle.
2. **`build()` takes the library.** `build(def, skeleton, outfit, item, library)`: the spec's four arguments plus `FigureLibrary` (`swatches`, `hair`, `headwear`, `items`, keyed by id as compiled content holds them), because figures has no global library (5a: no character or library in code). It is pure: it reads its arguments and writes nothing.
3. **Materials are palette colours.** `Built.materials` maps every material a part or decal names to `{ colour, gloss?, flat?, shade? }`: `colour` the swatch's palette index (5a's swatch), `shade` a tone step for the view (+1 a step lighter, −1 the shadow, −2 the deep: the offsets of the source's four-tone ramp, `[base + 1, base, base − 1, base − 2]`). Where the source makes a painter material from a swatch (`own`, `lighter`, `mark`, `flatTone`, the hair's tones, the ears' tones, a cap's seam), the part names a variant `<swatch>@<role>` resolved from its swatch (`build/materials.ts`): rib and inside are the cloth without its gloss (inside also `shade −1`); a stitch, a seam, a welt, a zip's teeth and pulls are `flat` at the tone the source picks (its light `+1`, mid `0`, deep `−2`; the moto's lit lapel edge, three steps up its light, `+4`); a flap, a waistband and the hair's lit and crest tones `shade +1`, the moto's folded lapels, collar and belt `matte`; the hair's parting `shade −2`, the scalp the skin's `shade −1`; a fade's tiers, which the painter mixes between hair and skin, the hair's `shade −1` (`fade1`) and the skin's `shade −1` (`fade2`); the ear's rim the skin `matte`, its bowl `matte, shade −1`; hoops `gloss 0.85`; pushed-up shades and their frame `matte, shade −1`. The puffer's alternate chamber name (`@baffle`, the same swatch, for the painter) is gone: every chamber is the cloth. The sheen and plait cells a rope or a shell splits into a second mesh (`lit`) stay geometry, in the lit variant, so a braid's plait reads in 3D too. The skirt's stain and the wide-legs' wash are painter washes and are cut: `fade` leaves the wide-legs garment, as 5a left out the skirt's `stain`.
4. **Fixed ppm.** `FIGURE_PPM = 73.8` texels a metre (FigureBuilder's `PPM`: twice the brand kit's 36.9, the density the source judges its figures at, density 2). `texels(want, least)` rounds to it. Marks the source draws per density take the density-2 form: a `sized` mark is one decal at its size, laces show under a hem, the chain's rope is its density-2 rope (two texels wide). The trousers' stitching, which the source draws tonal at density 2 and in the outfit's thread from density 3, is drawn once, in the outfit's `stitch` swatch when it names one (else the cloth's own light), so the field is not dead; a back pocket is its top edge (the density-3 outline is cut). The tail's cuffs, which the source builds only from 1.5 × PPM, are always built (a mesh has no texel floor).
5. **Decals are anchor points.** `Built.decals` are the source's point marks (`bone`, `at`, `normal`, `material`, `size?`, `when?`, `blink?`, `tag?`): the face's eyes, lids, brows and mouth, buttons, zips, stitches and the chain. M3.5d draws them as quads (spec: face decals are quads shown toward the camera). Prints (graphics, plates) are cut.
6. **`hairPieces`.** The hair an outfit wears stays its `{ hair, style }` garment. A hair garment with no `style` wears the figure's `hairPieces` instead, each style built in order in that garment's colour and under the outfit's cap; so a figure keeps its own hair across outfits, and each outfit says only its colour. A hair garment with a style wears that style alone; an outfit with no hair garment wears no hair. A style-less hair garment on a figure with no `hairPieces` is a content compile error at the garment (`figure.schema.ts`).
7. **`Garment` tightened.** 5a's `Readonly<Record<string, unknown>>` becomes the union of each garment's interface, the schemas typed by them; `hair.style` becomes optional (decision 6) and the wide-legs lose `fade` (decision 3). The eyes lose the drawn face's `white` and `lips`. Optional fields are typed `T | undefined` (zod's output under `exactOptionalPropertyTypes`). The source's default swatches take the library's kebab ids (`chrome-dark`, `holo-cyan`, `midsole`, `outsole`, `sole`, `chrome`, `mouth`, `lid`, `brow`, `eye-dark`); the shades' default frame, FigureBuilder's `jetBlack` (a SNEKPRTY crew swatch, not in its library), becomes `black`.
8. **Skin weights as the source.** A skinned vertex carries the source's weights (two or three bones across a joint, a hem's pivot as one more), summing to 1, for linear blend skinning. The spec's "two-bone linear blend" describes the common case; nothing is cut to two.
9. **The chain.** Its rope is the source's marks along the U at density 2, round the nape too; the nameplate, its print, its `pendant` anchor and the drop that grows to keep the plate on one cloth are cut, so the rope hangs at the item's `drop`.
10. **Anchors and clearance.** `Anchor` and `clearance` (FigureBuilder `motion.ts`) come here because `build()` returns them; the stands that resolve against them (`CHAIN`, `place`) are M3.5c's.
11. **Random characters.** `testing/random-character.ts`: `random(seed)` is FigureBuilder's mulberry32 (no `Math.random`); `randomCharacter(seed, library)` draws every slider over the source's ranges (the lookbook's narrower ones), a streetwear outfit layer by layer from the registry with the wardrobe's draws, each swatch field from the library swatches of a kind its role takes (or, where the library has none of those kinds, any swatch with no kind), a skin, a hair style with pieces, a cap three times in ten and an item half the time. The colourway's rules are cut with the colourway.
12. **Goldens from the source.** FigureBuilder `a1f7246` runs on Node 24 (a throwaway copy in the scratchpad with `.ts` import suffixes and JSON import attributes, not committed). `testing/__goldens__/figurebuilder-dress.json` (biome skips `__goldens__`; one part per line) holds, for the twelve outfits of `testing/golden-outfits.ts` on four bodies (between them every classic and streetwear garment, both kinds of headwear and every kind of hair piece), each part's bone, group, vertex and index counts, index sum, position and normal sums and skin-weight sum, each outfit's anchors and its clearance; the port matches them to 1e-9 relative. Excepted, as the test lists: the chain's plate and its `pendant` anchor (cut), and the pony's cuffs (added, decision 4). The script dressed each outfit with FigureBuilder's `dress(measure(sliders), skeleton(genes), skin, garments, swatches, PPM)`, its swatches a proxy that answers any id (geometry does not read colours), ids turned back into FigureBuilder's camelCase, and `clearance` from `motion.ts`.
13. **Strict TypeScript.** As 5a: the source's formulas and their order are kept; array reads go through `at` (which throws on a miss), meshes stay `number[]` (M3.5d makes the GPU buffers).

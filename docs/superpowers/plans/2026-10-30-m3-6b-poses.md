## Decisions (open to the Owner's veto)

1. **One contract owner.** `PoseDef.effectors` is `Partial<Record<EffectorId, EffectorTarget>>` and `poles` is `Partial<Record<PoleId, PoleTarget>>`, M3.6a's types from `solve/targets.ts`, not copies. The zod schema is typed against them (`z.ZodType<PoseDef>`), so a change there fails the typecheck here.
2. **No `id` field.** As `FigureDef` in M3.5a, a pose's id is its content file's name (`figure-poses/kneel.toml` is `kneel`), so a pose cannot disagree with its key. The spec's `PoseDef.id` is that key.
3. **What a pose may say** (compile checks, §6): effectors only from `EffectorId`, poles only from `PoleId`, `fk` joints only from `JOINTS` (all 27; the spec names clavicles, feet, hands and the root as the usual ones); `chest` and `head` take `rot` only (M3.6a's solve ignores a position there, so a pose that writes one is refused, not silently ignored); `pelvis` takes no `space` (its position is in root space); `weight` is 0 to 1; every number is finite (TOML allows `nan` and `inf`); every position (an effector's `pos`, the root's `pos`) lies within twice the figure's reach of the root. An unknown handle or joint is `unknownField` at its key; the others name the field and the value.
4. **Reach, and which figure a pose is checked against.** A figure's reach is the farthest any limb end gets from the root at rest: for each limb, the distance from the root to the limb's root joint plus its two bone lengths, the largest of the four, in figure units (divided by `k`). Poses are written in figure units and no content says which figures use a pose (figures name no poses; clips, M3.6c, and the sim name them), so the content compile checks every pose against the **reference figure**, the default sliders' skeleton (reach 1.84 figure units, so the bound is 3.69). Because positions are in figure units, a figure's reach there differs from the reference's only by its proportions (Wick and the keeper are within 10%, a test pins it), and the bound is twice the reach, so the check is a guard against typos, not a fit. A game or the Studio checks a pose against one figure with `poseProblems(pose, skeleton)`; the fixture test solves every library pose on both fixture figures (decision 9). A position in `chest` space is measured from the root through the reference figure's rest chest.
5. **Canonical spelling.** Numbers are rounded to 4 decimals with no `-0`, as the editor suite's codec writes numbers (`packages/editor-3d/src/codec/print.ts` `roundJsonNumber`, rounding in decimal notation as the editor kit's `roundTo` in `packages/editor-kit/src/widgets/widget-math.ts` does, so `2.675` rounds as it reads). The kit's helper is not imported: `figures` is renderer-free and ships in games (M3 spec §4.4), `editor-kit` is a private Svelte package that depends on Svelte and `ui`, and its `roundTo` is not a public export; the rule (4 decimals, decimal rounding, no `-0`) is mirrored in `pose/pose-spelling.ts` with a comment naming both. Defaults are left out (`space = "root"`, `weight = 1`, empty `poles` and `fk`, an empty `root`), keys go in schema order (handles in `EffectorId` order, joints in `JOINTS` order, fields `pos`, `rot`, `space`, `weight`), and the text is TOML written by `smol-toml` 1.9.0 (the version `content` pins). `printPose(pose)` writes it; `canonicalPose(pose)` is the value the printed text reads back as. A pose read back from its printed text equals `canonicalPose` of it, and printing that again is byte-equal. Every fixture pose is canonical.
6. **Mirror.** Left and right swap in handle ids (`footL`↔`footR`, `handL`↔`handR`, `kneeL`↔`kneeR`, `elbowL`↔`elbowR`) and in `fk` joints (`*.L`↔`*.R`, `hemL`↔`hemR`); positions and pole directions negate x; Euler rotations `(pitch, yaw, roll)` negate **yaw and roll** (reflecting through the body's middle plane conjugates a rotation by `diag(−1, 1, 1)`, which keeps a turn about x and reverses turns about y and z; the spec's "x and yaw negated" leaves out roll, which must flip too, and a test checks the matrices); the root's x and yaw negate. Negation never writes `-0`. `mirror(mirror(p))` equals `p` exactly.
7. **Blend.** `blend(a, b, t)`, with `t` clamped to `[0, 1]` (a `t` that is `NaN` throws a `RangeError`): `t = 0` returns `a` and `t = 1` returns `b` as written (an Euler triple has more than one spelling of a turn, so the ends are not recomputed). In between: positions and weights lerp (the math's `lerp`, `a + (b − a)·t`); rotations (effector `rot`, `fk`) slerp as quaternions and are read back as the source's Euler degrees; the root's yaw takes the shorter way round; pole directions lerp (the solver normalizes them; directions that cancel fall to its parallel-pole fallback). **A pose names only what it sets**, so a handle only one pose names blends against the layers below it, as §4.4.5 step 3 says for a channel one clip owns: it keeps its targets and its weight is scaled by that pose's share (`(1 − t)` for `a`, `t` for `b`), which `EffectorTarget.weight` already means ("blending from the base pose"). A pole one pose leaves out is that limb's default pole (M3.6a's `LIMBS`, root space); an `fk` joint or root field left out is zero. A field only one of the two sets inside a handle both name (a `rot` in one) is held from the one that sets it. A handle both name in different spaces throws a `RangeError` naming it: converting needs the chest frame of an evaluated pose, which the clip sampler has (§4.4.4 "keys in different spaces") and a pose does not. `about` is kept only at the ends.
8. **Applying a pose.** `applyPose(skeleton, base, pose)` returns the solve's input: `base` with `fk` (each joint's local rotation times the Euler turn: "extra" rotations) and `root` (`pos` in figure units in the figure's own frame, `yaw` in degrees, added to where the sim put the figure) applied, and `Targets` with the pose's effectors and poles, no aim and no pins. It is the single-pose input the Studio and the tests need; layering poses and clips is M3.6d's `evaluate`.
9. **The library** (`testing/fixtures/content/figure-poses/`, fixtures for tests and examples, none a game's): `stand` (hands hanging a little out and forward, chest and head level), `guard-high` (hands up before the face, elbows down and out, clavicles raised in `fk`), `guard-low` (hips lowered, hands at the belly, chest forward), `point` (the right hand out at shoulder height pointing ahead, the head turned to it, the body turned 15° by `root.yaw`), `carry` (both hands before the belly in `chest` space, the chest leaning back), `kneel` (hips down, the left foot forward flat, the right knee toward the floor), `hands-on-hips` (both hands where FigureBuilder's `handOnHip` places its hip hand, with its elbow pole and hand turn, mirrored for the left). Each solves on Wick and the keeper with the default rig limits, every effector `ok` and nothing limited.
10. **`hands-on-hips` against FigureBuilder.** `place()` gives the wrist in the pelvis bone's rest frame; with the pelvis at rest that is the root frame shifted by the pelvis's rest position. Wick's and the keeper's places differ (the keeper's longer arm slides the hand down the hip), so the pose holds the mean of the two in figure units, and the test asserts each figure's solved hands land within **0.01 figure units** (1 cm on a 1.62 m figure) of where FigureBuilder places that figure's hands. The recorded pelvis rest heights are checked against AF's skeletons, so a drift between the two rigs fails the test rather than the tolerance absorbing it.

## Decisions (open to the Owner's veto)

1. **Units.** Effector positions are figure units in their `space` (`root`: the root joint's frame, the figure's own +z forward; `chest`: the chest joint's frame), multiplied by `k = height / 1.62` at solve time. Pins and aim points are world positions (they come from the view and the sim), not scaled. Euler rotations are degrees, `(pitch, yaw, roll)`, composed as the source's `euler` (yaw · pitch · roll).
2. **Rotation handles are local.** `pelvis.rot`, `chest.rot` and `head.rot` set their joints' local rotations (as the source's `base()` writes them), so `space` does not apply to them; `pelvis.pos` is the pelvis joint's position in root space. `chest` and `head` take no position (`pos` on them is ignored; M3.6b's pose compile may refuse it). `weight` blends from the base pose: positions linearly, rotations by slerp.
3. **Splits.** `chest.rot` is shared 0.3/0.7 by `spine` and `chest` (the source's posture split); `head.rot` is shared 0.4/0.6 by `neck` and `head` (the source's head split). A head aim replaces `head.rot` (spec: "an `AimTarget` with `part: 'head'` takes a look-at instead").
4. **End rotations.** A foot or hand effector's `rot` is the end's world rotation in its `space`. Without one, a re-solved foot keeps its base world rotation (a planted foot stays flat while the hips move, as the source's `footTurn`); a hand keeps its local rotation (the source's reach and flinch pass no end turn).
5. **When a limb solves.** A foot solves when it has an effector position, a pin, or the pelvis effector moved the hips (then its target is its base world position: "the legs re-solve so the feet stay on their targets"). A hand solves when it has an effector position, a pin or a hand aim. A pin outranks a hand aim, and a hand aim outranks an effector position. Limbs are never one another's ancestors, so all four solve from the one world the torso leaves.
6. **Default poles** are the source's, in root space: knees `[0, 0, 1]` (forward), elbows `[x·0.7, −0.5, −0.5]` (back, down and out; `x` is +1 for the left side, −1 for the right). A `PoleTarget` gives the direction in its `space`.
7. **Aim.** A head aim turns the head's forward axis (+z) from the head joint toward the point; a chest aim the chest's. The aim is one swing `A` in the pelvis's frame, an axis and an angle (revised after review: the first version split yaw and pitch, which missed steep looks by up to 5° inside the limits and broke near straight down). Each joint takes `A` to the power of its share (head: spine 0.1, chest 0.2, neck 0.3, head 0.4; chest: spine 0.3, chest 0.7), carried into its own frame through its parent's turn when the aim began, so the aimed part turns by exactly `A` and each joint by the running sum of the shares before it; then each joint is clamped. Steps measure the shortest swing left, divide it by the shares still free (so joints left unclamped take up what the clamped ones cannot), and keep it only if the angle left shrinks; a step that does not help is retried at half size, down to 1/256, and the steps stop under 1e-9 rad or after 40. The face's heading (its yaw in the pelvis's frame) is held inside the aim joints' yaw bounds summed (115° for the head with the default limits): per-joint limits alone do not bound it (a yaw about a pitched chest, with roll, compounds; the first version ended 0.33° past, the axis-angle version up to 35° past), so a point past the bound is aimed at as if it sat on it, a step that takes the face further past is refused, and the aimed part (`head` or `chest`) is then listed in `report.limited`. Chest aims solve before head aims whatever their order in `aim`; aims of one part keep their order. A hand aim points the arm straight from the shoulder at the point (the hand on the shoulder-to-point ray at full reach) and lines the hand up with the forearm. `report.reached.aim` is the worst aim's angle in degrees, `ok` within 0.5° (I-e); a weighted aim is judged against its blended goal (a torso part against the direction that weight of the way round from its forward axis before the aim to the point; a hand against its blended target).
8. **Limits, per joint.** Elbows and knees are hinges: the bend about the joint's local x (flexion: a knee bends back, an elbow forward), clamped to `[min, max]`, onto the bound nearer round the circle (a knee at −170° goes to 150°). Shoulders and hips are cones: the bone's swing from hanging straight down, clamped to `cone`, keeping its twist. The chest's twist (yaw) and pitch ranges are shared by `spine` and `chest` by the posture split (the spine gets 0.3 of each bound, the chest 0.7), so the torso as a whole stays inside them; the neck clamps yaw and pitch, the head yaw, pitch and roll, each read as the source's euler of the joint's local rotation. Every rotation has two eulers, (p, y, r) and (180° − p, y + 180°, r + 180°); both are clamped and the one needing the smaller correction wins, so a head pitched 96° clamps to (30°, 0°, 0°), not to a flipped yaw and roll. `limitExcess` is that correction in degrees. Clamping rotates only past the bound back to it, so a joint inside its limits is never touched. `report.limited` lists every joint any clamp moved during the solve, in `JOINTS` order.
9. **Report units.** `error` is in figure units for positions (limbs, pins, pelvis) and in degrees for rotations (chest, head, a rotation-only pelvis) and aim. A position is `ok` within 1e-6 figure units, a rotation within 1e-6°, an aim within 0.5°. A head handle that a head aim replaced is not judged (no `reached.head`).
10. **Tuned limits** (fixture `figure-rigs/default.toml`, M3.5a's content, values changed here; reasons in step 7's log): elbow and knee 0° to 150° (spec), shoulder cone 170° (an arm points almost straight up), hip cone 120° (a deep sit or a climb), chest twist ±35° (spec) and pitch −20° to 30°, neck yaw ±35° and pitch −20° to 25°, head yaw ±45°, pitch −25° to 30°, roll ±25°. The spec's "neck yaw ±70°, pitch −40° to +50°" is read as the head on the chest, now split over the neck and the head; the whole turn from the hips is at most 10.5 + 24.5 + 35 + 45 = 115°. They are starting values for the Studio's drags (EDS.9b).
11. **Cost.** The source's allocating `twoBoneIK` and `fk` are used as they are (S7-Q8: typed-array buffers only if it misses). A `bench` vitest project (as `packages/physics`) measures and prints, never asserts; it is not in `pnpm check`. The first measure missed 0.1 ms (step 9's log), so: the aim's steps walk only the aimed chain (`chain-world.ts`, the same products as `fk`), the base pose is walked only for a re-solved foot, and `mmul` and `mul3` are written out element by element instead of with `flatMap` (the source's sums in its order: bit-identical on 20,000 seeded products, and every M3.5a test still passes). M3.5a's review made the same change; after the merge its inline form is kept (as fast as this branch's, within noise). No typed arrays.
12. **Exports for the Studio.** Besides the spec's types and `solve`, `jointLimits`, `JointLimit`, `limitExcess`, `hingeFlexion`, `swingAngle` and `eulerOf` are exported, so EDS.9b can show a handle at its limit (amber) from the same readings the solver clamps by.

## Why

A camera 25° down and 5 m back stands outside the room or inside a wall most of the time, so the near walls, the ceiling and any tall prop between it and the player would cover the action. The occluder rule takes them out of the way each frame, smoothly and without flicker while the player turns the camera, and the framing check lets an author prove a view keeps its room in the frame at the narrowest and widest screens.

## Decisions (agent defaults, for the Owner's veto)

1. **The facing test is the eye's side of the wall's inside face**, as the spec's parenthesis says ("the camera is on its outer side"): a wall is cut when `(eye − a) · n ≤ 0` (`n` the inward normal, on the left of `a → b`). This is the editor's `facesEye`, so the editor's preview and the game agree.
2. **A wall the camera's line to the player crosses is cut too** (The Last Light's `wallHidden`): the segment from the eye to the player's head or feet goes from the wall's inside to its outside through the wall's solid (its span plus 0.6 m at each end, its height, its thickness), unless it ends in the doorway: within 0.12 m of the inside face, or anywhere in the wall's depth up to 0.12 m past its outside face (revised in step 10: measured from the inside face alone, a 0.2 m wall cut both pieces either side of a player in its doorway). Through the solid, not only the face: the sweep found a line that passes over the face's top edge and comes down through the wall's thickness (decision revised, see the log). In one convex room it never adds a wall (the eye outside a wall is already the facing test); it matters across a doorway, where the eye stands in the next room and that room's copy of the partition faces it. Without it D3-h fails for two rooms.
3. **Hysteresis is 5° of camera turn and errs toward cutting.** A standing wall is cut the moment the facing test says so; a cut wall comes back only when the facing test and the crossing test are both clear and would stay clear with the eye turned 5° either way about the look point. So a wall never stands while the eye is outside it (D3-h holds in every frame), and turning back and forth across the edge does not flicker it. **This departs from D3-h's "the cut-away walls are exactly those whose inside face points away from the camera"** (for the Owner's veto, review of #249): the hysteresis keeps a wall cut for up to 5° after the eye is back inside it, and the crossing test (decision 2) cuts a wall the eye faces when the line to a player in the next room passes through it. "Exactly" holds for the rule's answer at a pose read with no history in one room (the settled sweep); the continuous sweep proves the part that matters, that no frame has a ray to the player blocked by a mesh at full opacity. The hysteresis covers turning the camera, not walking: a player walking the eye across a wall's line cuts it at once and restores it after 5° of equivalent clearance, as the spec asks for turning only (review item 8, recorded).
4. **One fade, 150 ms, linear in a 0-to-1 progress**, both ways: opacity is `1 − progress × (1 − floor)`, with floor 0 for `cut` and 0.25 for `fade`. A cut wall's trim shows while the wall is not fully shown. Reduced motion (`prefers-reduced-motion`, read each frame) jumps, and so do the pass's first step and the first after a reset, a resync or a camera cut (`CameraPose.cut`), since nothing was drawn to fade from (step 10). A mesh at opacity 1 is `alphaMode: 'opaque'` (it writes depth); below 1 it is `'blend'`; at 0 it is hidden.
5. **What is registered, and how.** Static scenery is built by the game's `scene` hook, which now gets a third argument, `occluders`: `occluders.wall({ a, b, height, thickness?, trim?, color, occlude? })` builds a wall (a box whose inside face lies on `a → b`, `thickness` 0.2 m outward) and its floor trim (0.12 m high), each with its own material; `occluders.ceiling(node)` hides `node` whenever the angled camera runs (spec §4.2 (2)); `occluders.prop(mesh, { bounds, occlude? })` fades or cuts a mesh whose world `bounds` (`{ min: [x, y, z], max: [x, y, z] }`, the camera view's `bounds` shape) the eye-to-head or eye-to-feet segment crosses. `occlude` defaults: `cut` for walls, `fade` for props taller than 1.5 m, `never` for shorter props (§4.2). The opacity is the prop's material's, so a prop whose material is shared (a box view's, a loaded model's: both register a copy maker in `views/shared-material.ts`) gets its own copy; a material another prop already fades is refused (step 10). Walls, trims and boxes are one unit cube scaled, freed with the renderer (`dispose`). `occluders.box(bounds, color)` builds a lit box with its own material and no rule (a floor, a ceiling, a pillar), so a game or an example needs no `@pixi/3d` import for blockout scenery (added in step 8: examples import only public entries). Floors are not registered: they never go.
6. **One owner for `occlude`.** `OCCLUDES`, `Occlude` and `occludeSchema` live in render-pixi3d's pure `./camera-view` entry; editor-3d's sector format imports them instead of keeping its own list (as M3.4b did for the view).
7. **The pass needs the camera's pose.** `CameraRig.pose()` returns the eye, the look point and the followed position as last written (`undefined` before the first `follow`). The player's head is the followed position plus `Pixi3dCameraOptions.height` (1.62 m by default, the spec's figure). Without `options.camera` nothing is cut or faded (there is no player to keep in view).
8. **D3-g's check is a function.** `framingMisses({ view, outline, at, aspects, stepDeg })` lists every heading (every `stepDeg`, 15 by default) and aspect (4:3 and 21:9 by default) at which the player's focus (`focusHeight` above `at`, not the camera's look point, which bounds may hold elsewhere: step 10) or a corner of the floor outline falls outside the frame (|NDC| > 1 or behind the eye), with the player at `at` (default the outline's centre). base-3d's views (M3.7b) run it in their tests; here it runs on the example's view and on fixtures.
9. **D3-h here runs on fixtures**, a one-room box and two rooms joined by a doorway, because `games/base-3d` is M3.7b. The player stands 0.3 m in from each corner (a body's half-width) and at the centre. Only walls and props are tested: the floor is `never` and a ray to the feet ends on it.

## Findings for the Owner

- **Recorded from the review of #249, not changed:** (a) the hysteresis covers turning, not walking (decision 3; the spec asks for turning). (b) The facing rule exists twice, in render-pixi3d's `occluder-rule.ts` and in editor-3d's `facesEye` (`view/mesh/mesh-pick.ts`); both are one line over different shapes (a plan span, a surface's first corner), and moving the rule into `./camera-view` would pull `Vec3` and the slab test into the pure entry and change the editor's surface model, which is not trivial, so it waits for a task that unifies the editor's mesh with the renderer's walls. (c) `WallSpan` has no base height: every wall stands on y 0, so a wall on a raised floor or a mezzanine would need one (M6 sectors).

- **An extension's sim plugins never reach the sim.** `resolveExtensions` (runtime) returns `simPlugins`, but nothing consumes it, so `render3dExtension.sim` (the heading's plugin, M3.4b) is not in a game's sim: the example's first boot failed with `unknown component "camera-heading": register it in a plugin`. The example names `cameraHeadingPlugin` in its own `sim.plugins`, with a comment. The fix is in core runtime (boot passes the extensions' plugins to the sim), out of this task's scope; worth a row or a line in M3.7a, whose base-3d sim needs it.

- **D3-g and the default view.** With the player at the room's centre, the Owner's default view (25° down, 5 m back) keeps every floor corner of a square room in the frame at 4:3 and 21:9 only up to about 3 m across; at 4 m a near corner falls 47% past the frame's edge (the floor within about 2 m behind the player is below the frame). base-3d's rooms (M3.7b) will need views set further back (a 4 m room: 7 m at 25°, 8 m at 30°; a 5 m room: 8 to 10 m) or D3-g read as the walls the view frames from where the player stands, not the whole outline. Measured by the scan in the log; the check is `framingMisses`.

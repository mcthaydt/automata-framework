## Why

Every 3D game needs the Owner's camera (24 mm, 25° down, 5 m back, turned freely) and a stick that moves the character away from the camera at any heading. The heading changes gameplay, so it is sim state, deterministic in four engines; the picture is presentation only. The 3D level editor already carries a copy of the view's numbers and the heading maths (ED3D.4c); this task makes `render-pixi3d` their one owner.

## Decisions (agent defaults, for the Owner's veto)

1. **Two entries besides the main one.** `./camera-view` is the pure entry the spec names (no Pixi, no `@pixi/3d`): the `camera-view` zod schema with its defaults and ranges, `fovOf`, `LENS_MM`, and the heading maths. `./sim` is the sim entry, declared in `package.json` `"automata": { "simEntries": ["./sim"] }` and checked by `pnpm boundaries` (a new root in the root script): the schema and heading maths live there (zod and integers only), and `./camera-view` re-exports them, so there is one definition. `fovOf` needs `atan`, which the sim may not call, so it stays outside the sim folder.
2. **A view's id is its file name.** Content items take their id from the file (`content/camera-views/hall.toml` is `hall`), so `cameraViewSchema` validates the file's fields and `CameraView` is `{ id } & ` its output. Bounds are `{ min = [x, y, z], max = [x, y, z] }`, as the editor's `Box3`; `min` must not pass `max`.
3. **The heading state is one component, `camera-heading`** (`view`, `heading`, `rotate`, `latched`, `latchHeading`, `latchX`, `latchY`), on the player. It is a render component, so the renderer reads `view` and `heading` from the rows. `rotate` is copied from the view on entry, so the `camera-turn` system needs no content lookup.
4. **`camera-turn { units }` takes any integer and the system clamps the tick's total to ±8,192**, then wraps at 65,536. Several commands in one tick add up before the clamp. Every entity with `camera-heading` turns (a game has one player; a split-screen game would need a source field, which nothing asks for yet).
5. **The latch (M3R-D15).** Entering a different view while the stick is held latches the mapping to the heading before the entry and remembers the stick. It lets go when the stick is centred or points more than 90° from where it pointed at the latch (an integer dot product below 0: exactly 90° holds). Entering another view while latched keeps the first heading. **A player's camera turn lets go of a latch** ("they asked for the new heading"); entering a view with the stick centred does not latch.
6. **The mapper's output is Q16.16 integers.** `mapStick({ x, y }, heading)` takes the stick as `Input.stick()` gives it (integers −127..127, y positive down, M4-D2) and returns the world direction `{ x, z }` scaled by the stick's length, each rounded to the nearest Q16.16 unit **halves away from zero** (as `quantizeStick`), so the opposite stick gives exactly the opposite direction. Stick up gives `(sin h, cos h)`, stick right the camera's right, `(−cos h, sin h)` (`@pixi/3d` is right-handed: a camera looking toward +z has +x on its left). **A stick past full push (a keyboard's or a square gate's diagonal) is scaled back to unit length** (`Math.sqrt`, correctly rounded), so a diagonal is never faster; GAME-3D need not normalise. Integers keep the replay and the hash exact.
7. **`angledCamera` takes the camera it writes and the aspect.** The spec's `angledCamera({ views, initial })` gains `camera` (the `View3D`'s `Camera3D`, the only thing it writes), `aspect` (for the middle-third test of `frame`), `reducedMotion` (default: the player's `prefers-reduced-motion`, read each frame, as `camera2d`) and `warn` (default `console.warn`).
8. **Eases.** `activate(id, 'ease')` blends the camera's eye and look point from where they were to the new view's live pose over 400 ms (smoothstep); reduced motion makes it a cut. **A cut always snaps, even into the view already active** (a new sim or a resync in the same view), and an ease never starts from a pose taken before the first `follow`. `heading(yaw, 'ease')` turns the shown heading by the shorter arc over 250 ms (smoothstep; reduced motion jumps); `heading(yaw)` (track) shows at once when no ease runs. **While an ease runs, a track moves the ease's end by the shorter-arc difference** (the renderer tracks every frame, so the same target lets it finish, and a small player turn during a view entry's ease shifts it instead of snapping). The view's own heading arrives through the sim (`heading(yaw, 'ease')` on entry), so D3-a's "moves the camera to the view's heading" lands within 250 ms of the 400 ms blend.
9. **The follow.** The focus (the followed position raised by `focusHeight`) approaches its target with `1 − e^(−t / lag)` a frame (lag 0.18 s) and is clamped inside `bounds`. Nothing is seeded until the first `follow` (the renderer draws before the player's row exists), so the first framed frame snaps. **Reduced motion makes the lag instant** (spec §8; revised after review of #245, which found the first reading kept the lag).
10. **`frame(lock)`** moves the focus target up to 35% of the way toward the lock target, clamped by bounds; when that would put the player outside the middle third of the frame (either axis, at the rig's aspect) the share is cut by bisection until the player is inside. `frame()` clears it.
11. **The renderer wiring.** `Pixi3dOptions.camera` (optional) is `{ views, initial, follow }`, where `follow` is the component id that holds the followed entity's `x`, `y`, `z`. The renderer finds the row with `camera-heading` and that component, calls `activate(view, 'cut')` on the first row with a view and `'ease'` on a change, `heading(yaw, 'ease')` when the entered view sets `yawDeg` (read through the rig's `viewOf`, so `setViews` is seen), else `heading(yaw)` every frame with **the latest tick's heading exactly, not interpolated** (§4.2: nothing eases between the finger and the camera; a turn is at most a tick old), `follow` with the interpolated position, and `update`. A row whose view is still `''` (the component's start) has no view yet. Before the first layout the aspect is taken as 16:9 (a 0 × 0 view would make it NaN). A game that passes no `camera` keeps M3.4a's `scene` camera.
12. **Content and extension.** `cameraViewContent` (`camera-view`, folder `camera-views`, sent to the sim: it decides `rotate` and the entry heading) and `cameraHeadingPlugin` join `render3dExtension`.
13. **Editor-3d consumes the owner.** `CameraViewSpec`, `CAMERA_DEFAULTS`, `CAMERA_RANGES`, `cameraViewSpec`, `TURN_UNITS`, `wrapHeading`, `turnHeading` and `degToHeading` leave editor-3d; `followCamera` and `roomView` take `CameraView`; its tests import the owner's. editor-3d's `dragTurn` stays (an editor tool's drag, unclamped) until M4.12's `turnUnitsFromPixels` merges; its compass conversions (`headingToYaw`, `yawToHeading`, `headingToDeg`) are the editor camera's and stay.

**Design (agent defaults, for the Owner's veto).** `CameraRig`'s interface stays, so the renderer, the driver and the sim side do not change.

- `@pmndrs/klipp` pinned exactly at `0.16.0` (MIT; core has no renderer; `three`, `react`, `camera-controls` and `@react-three/fiber` are optional peers, none installed). The Last Light pins `0.14.0` (`92e6704`, `src/render/camera-rig.ts`), whose `klipp.addCamera` is gone in 0.16 (`new VirtualCamera(name, { priority })` then `klipp.add(camera)`).
- One `Klipp`, one `VirtualCamera` per `camera-view`, the active one `active = true`. `activate(id, 'ease')` sets the default blend to `{ curve: BlendCurves.easeInOut, time: 0.4 }` before the switch; `cut` and reduced motion set `BlendCurves.cut`.
- Each camera's body is `OrbitFollowBody(target, { orbitStyle: 'sphere', radius: view.distance, bindingMode: 'worldSpace', damping: view.lag })`: `vertical` is `view.tiltDeg`, `horizontal` is `180 − heading` (klipp's horizontal 0 puts the eye at +z and 90 at −x; ours looks along `(sin h, cos h)`). `heading(yaw)` (track) is `horizontal.setValue`; `heading(yaw, 'ease')` is klipp's `easeAxisTo` with the axis damping set for about 250 ms.
